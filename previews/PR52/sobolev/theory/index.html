Theory: Sobolev (derivative) supervision

This page states the method implemented by lib/L2OSobolev as an optimization problem, the conditions under which its derivative labels are the derivatives of the solution map, and the approximation results of the paper. Equation numbers in the form "eq. (n) of [R26]" refer to the paper below; numbers in the form (S n) are local to this page.

[R26] A. W. Rosemberg, J. Dias Garcia, R. Bent, P. Van Hentenryck. Masked Sobolev Training for Feasibility-Reliable Optimization Proxies. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026.

Setting and notation

A parametric optimization problem with parameters $p ∈ \mathcal P ⊂ ℝ^d$ and decision variables $x ∈ ℝ^n$,

\[g(p) = x^\star(p) ∈ \arg\min_x \; \{\, f(x; p) \;:\; c(x; p) = 0,\; h(x; p) ≤ 0 \,\}, \tag{S1}\]

defines the solution map $g : \mathcal P → ℝ^n$. An optimization proxy $\hat g_θ : ℝ^d → ℝ^n$ (a neural network with weights $θ$) approximates $g$. Its Jacobian is $D\hat g_θ(p) ∈ ℝ^{n×d}$ and the solver sensitivity is $Dg(p) = ∂x^\star/∂p ∈ ℝ^{n×d}$.

Derivative labels from KKT sensitivity

Collect the primal-dual solution in $s = (x, ν, μ)$ (multipliers of the equalities and inequalities). A KKT point satisfies $F(s^\star, p) = 0$ with

\[F(s, p) = \begin{pmatrix} ∇_x f(x;p) + ∇_x c(x;p)^⊤ ν + ∇_x h(x;p)^⊤ μ \\ c(x;p) \\ \operatorname{diag}(μ)\, h(x;p) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad μ ≥ 0,\; h(x;p) ≤ 0 . \tag{S2}\]

Theorem (Fiacco, 1976). Let $f, c, h$ be $C^2$ near $(x^\star, p_0)$ and assume at $s^\star = s^\star(p_0)$: the linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ), the second-order sufficient conditions (SOSC) and strict complementarity ($μ_j > 0$ for every active inequality). Then on a neighborhood of $p_0$ the KKT point $s^\star(p)$ is unique, continuously differentiable, the active set does not change, and

\[∇_p s^\star = -\big(∇_s F(s^\star, p)\big)^{-1}\, ∇_p F(s^\star, p). \tag{S3}\]

(S3) is eq. (1) of [R26]; $Dg(p)$ is its $x$ block. Computing it is one linear solve with the KKT Jacobian per direction:

  • forward mode (Jacobian–vector product): $Dg(p)\,v = -[∇_s F^{-1} ∇_p F\, v]_x$, one solve per parameter direction $v$;
  • reverse mode (vector–Jacobian product): solve $∇_s F^⊤ z = (w, 0, 0)$ and return $w^⊤ Dg(p) = -z^⊤ ∇_p F$, one solve per output seed $w$.

DiffOptSensitivities computes these with DiffOpt.jl; a full Jacobian costs n reverse (or d forward) solves, a directional label one solve.

Linear and convex quadratic programs. For LPs and convex QPs with parameters in the right-hand side and linear cost, $g$ is piecewise affine: inside a critical region (fixed non-degenerate active set) $Dg$ is constant, and on region boundaries $g$ is not differentiable. Sensitivities are therefore exact local derivatives at almost every sample, and carry no information about neighbouring regions.

Directional labels. Instead of the full $Dg(p_k)$, a sample may carry a pullback $(w_k, Dg(p_k)^⊤ w_k)$ (PullbackLabels, one reverse solve) or a pushforward $(v_k, Dg(p_k) v_k)$ (PushforwardLabels, one forward solve). With one-hot seeds these are a random row or column of the Jacobian: a sparse, stochastic subset of its entries chosen when the data is labeled.

Masked Sobolev loss

Given tuples $(p_i, g(p_i), Dg(p_i))$, the first-order masked Sobolev loss is

\[\mathcal L(θ) = \frac1N \sum_{i=1}^N \ell\big(\hat g_θ(p_i), g(p_i)\big) + \frac{λ}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \ell_d\big(\mathcal M ⊙ D\hat g_θ(p_i),\, \mathcal M ⊙ Dg(p_i)\big), \tag{S4}\]

(eq. (2) of [R26]) with the normalized masked squared error

\[\ell_d\big(\mathcal M ⊙ D\hat g_θ(p_i), \mathcal M ⊙ Dg(p_i)\big) = \frac{\big\|\mathcal M ⊙ \big(D\hat g_θ(p_i) - Dg(p_i)\big)\big\|_F^2}{\|\mathcal M\|_0} \tag{S5}\]

(eq. (3) of [R26]). If $\mathcal M$ is drawn uniformly among the masks with exactly $k$ ones, each entry is kept with probability $k/(nd)$, so

\[\mathbb E_{\mathcal M}[\ell_d] = \frac{1}{nd}\, \big\|D\hat g_θ(p_i) - Dg(p_i)\big\|_F^2 \tag{S6}\]

(eq. (4) of [R26]): $λ$ sets the strength of derivative supervision and the mask density only sets how many matching conditions enter one update. [R26] uses the mask as an algorithmic control: dense matching can over-constrain the proxy where neighbouring samples have conflicting active-set sensitivities, and keeping 5–10% of the entries gave lower infeasibility on AC-OPF (Table 2 of [R26]; its reported settings use densities of 5–25%, weights $λ ∈ [0.1, 4.35]$ and mini-batches of 32).

The same estimator applies to directional labels: for pullbacks the mask acts on the $d$ entries of $Dg^⊤ w$, for pushforwards on the $n$ entries of $Dg\, v$. With seeds $w$ uniform over the $n$ unit vectors,

\[\mathbb E_w\Big[\tfrac1d \big\|(D\hat g_θ - Dg)^⊤ w\big\|_2^2\Big] = \frac{1}{nd}\big\|D\hat g_θ - Dg\big\|_F^2 , \tag{S7}\]

and likewise for one-hot directions $v$. The same holds for seeds drawn uniformly on the unit sphere, since then $\mathbb E[w w^⊤] = I/n$ (and $\mathbb E[v v^⊤] = I/d$). Directional labels are therefore unbiased for the dense loss in expectation over the labeling, and a random mask on top of them keeps this property.

Structured masks. A mask that keeps $k$ of the $d$ columns of the Jacobian uniformly at random (RandomColumnMask) keeps each entry with probability $k/d$; one that keeps $k$ of the $n$ rows (RandomRowMask) with probability $k/n$. Every mask of either kind has the same $\|\mathcal M\|_0$ ($kn$ or $kd$), so normalizing by it as in (S5) again gives the dense loss (S6) in expectation. With full-Jacobian labels, only the selected columns $D\hat g_θ e_j$ ($k$ Jacobian–vector products) or rows $e_i^⊤ D\hat g_θ$ ($k$ vector–Jacobian products) of the proxy are needed. The loss computes only those, except for row masks with CentralDifferences, which differentiates column by column and therefore computes the full Jacobian.

Composition with other methods

[R26] describes the value term of (S4) as interchangeable: the derivative term complements self-supervised objectives (its "semi-self-supervised" variant) and can be applied before or through a repair/completion layer. SobolevRegularized implements exactly this by replacing the first sum of (S4) by the loss of the wrapped method $\mathcal L_{\text{inner}}$:

\[\mathcal L(θ) = \mathcal L_{\text{inner}}(θ) + \frac{λ}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \ell_d\big(\mathcal M ⊙ D\hat x_θ(p_i),\, \mathcal M ⊙ Dg(p_i)\big), \tag{S8}\]

where $\hat x_θ$ is either the raw primal output of the proxy ($\hat g_θ$, the default) or the completed primal prediction $\hat x_θ(p) = \mathrm{complete}(p, \hat g_θ(p))$ (through_completion = true, chain rule through the completion layer). With SupervisedMSE as the inner method, (S8) is exactly (S4) with $\ell$ the mean squared error.

Computing the proxy derivatives (nested differentiation)

The weights are trained by differentiating (S8) with respect to $θ$, and (S8) itself contains input derivatives of $\hat g_θ$: second-order mixed derivatives $∂_θ ∂_p \hat g_θ$ are needed. Three backends are provided:

  • LuxNestedAD: exact $D\hat g_θ$ via Lux's batched_jacobian, jacobian_vector_product and vector_jacobian_product, which Lux differentiates again under Zygote (forward-over-reverse rules);
  • ZygoteNestedAD: exact $D\hat g_θ$ of a Flux model or any other Zygote-differentiable callable via Zygote pullbacks (reverse over reverse): $D\hat g_θ^⊤ w$ is one pullback, the full Jacobian one pullback of width $nB$ with the $n$ unit seeds, and $D\hat g_θ v$ the Jacobian applied to $v$;
  • CentralDifferences: $D\hat g_θ v ≈ (\hat g_θ(p + hv) - \hat g_θ(p - hv))/2h$ with truncation error $O(h^2 \|D^3 \hat g_θ\|)$ and rounding error $O(ε(\|\hat g_θ\| + \|D\hat g_θ\|\,\|p\|_∞)/h)$, balanced by the default $h = ε^{1/3}\max(1, \|P\|_∞)$. The outer AD only differentiates forward passes (works for any proxy and any AD, and through completion layers); its weight gradient is that of the approximate loss, which agrees with the exact nested-AD gradient up to the same $O(h^2)$ error.

The networks of [R26] use ReLU, sigmoid and LeakyReLU activations. For piecewise-linear activations $D\hat g_θ$ exists almost everywhere and is differentiable in $θ$ almost everywhere, so the loss is still trainable, but $D\hat g_θ$ is not Lipschitz and the Jacobian-Lipschitz condition of the next section does not hold for the proxy; Theorem 3 then does not apply as stated.

Approximation bounds (regular regime)

Let $\mathcal P$ be compact and convex, $\mathcal T = \{p_i\}_{i=1}^N ⊂ \mathcal P$ and $δ = \sup_{p ∈ \mathcal P} \min_i \|p - p_i\|$ the covering radius. Theorems 1–3 of [R26] compare proxies that interpolate the samples exactly. If $g$ and $\hat g_θ$ are Lipschitz with constants $L_g$ and $L_{\hat g}$, value interpolation bounds the value error by $δ$ (Theorem 1). If both are $C^1$ on $\mathcal P$ with Jacobians Lipschitz with constants $M_g$ and $M_{\hat g}$, Jacobian interpolation bounds the Jacobian error by $δ$ (Theorem 2), and interpolating both values and Jacobians bounds the value error by $δ^2$ (Theorem 3). $δ$ can be estimated on a test set with covering_radius.

Theorem 3 follows from a first-order Taylor expansion around the nearest sample: zeroth- and first-order terms cancel and the remainders are bounded by $\tfrac12 M\|h\|^2$. These are approximation bounds for idealized interpolants, not convergence results for training with (S4): the practical loss does not reach exact interpolation and masking matches a subset of entries per update. The Jacobian-Lipschitz condition holds for $g$ inside a region where the active set is constant and the Fiacco conditions hold (Remark 1 of [R26]); networks with smooth activations (tanh, softplus) satisfy it on compact sets. Across active-set changes $Dg$ jumps, and derivative supervision only helps when neighbouring sensitivities are similar.

Failure modes

  • Degenerate samples (weakly active constraints, $p$ on a critical-region boundary): no derivative exists; the KKT matrix is singular or the result is one one-sided derivative. label_errors compares labels with central differences of the solver map and flags such samples (for example an LP sampled at a kink), and generate_data(...; max_label_error) drops them.
  • Non-unique or local solutions (dual-degenerate LPs, nonconvex NLPs): the derivative may belong to another solution branch than the value label. generate_data drops samples whose oracle solution differs from the value label (consistency_atol).
  • Highly local sensitivities: when active sets change on a scale smaller than the sample spacing, $Dg$ carries little information about neighbours; [R26] observed less benefit than value-only training in a mean–variance portfolio study. Lower weight or mask density, or do not use the term.
  • Parameters only in a linear cost (LP): the optimal vertex is constant inside each region, so the sensitivities are zero and carry no interpolation information ([R26], Sec. 5.3).
  • Failed samples kept (drop_failed = false): their labels are NaN, and NaN entries poison the loss even where the mask is zero ($0 · \mathrm{NaN} = \mathrm{NaN}$); keep the default or filter them.
  • Integer decisions: the solution map is piecewise constant; sensitivities are zero or undefined. SobolevRegularized does not refuse MixedInteger problems if the inner method supports them, but the labels are not meaningful there.

Deviations from [R26], and why

  • The value term of (S4) is the wrapped method's loss (S8), so the same regularizer composes with supervised, self-supervised and completion-based methods by dispatch. With SupervisedMSE it is exactly (S4).
  • One mask is drawn per loss evaluation and shared by the batch, matching the single $\mathcal M$ of eq. (2) of [R26].
  • Directional (pullback/pushforward) labels are an addition: they record one solver sensitivity solve per sample instead of a full Jacobian, and are unbiased for the dense loss with one-hot seeds (S7).
  • CentralDifferences approximates $D\hat g_θ$ (error $O(h^2)$) to support any ML framework and AD; use LuxNestedAD (Lux) or ZygoteNestedAD (Flux or any Zygote-differentiable callable) for exact derivatives.
  • DiffOpt labels default to reverse mode: with DiffOpt 0.6 we measured wrong forward-mode sensitivities for parameters inside inequality constraints (reverse mode and DiffOpt 0.5 agree with finite differences); the test suite tracks this as a known upstream issue.
